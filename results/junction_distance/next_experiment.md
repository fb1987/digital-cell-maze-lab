# After V12J — preserve mechanisms, test robustness pressure

**Proposal only. Not registered or launched.** V12J's main budget is closed.
Do not extend it, rerun it, resume G/H/I, or use inspected J outcomes as holdout
validation. Preserve all old populations/RNG and all thirteen fixed random-origin
models, including both parents, the shared ancestor and the68-gene random draw.
Exclude all150,776 whole layouts through J, including fixtures and duplicate
shape-labelled inputs (the exclusion set contains unique whole layouts).

J makes a single distance-limit diagnosis inadequate. Eight-edge-prefix paired
forks exist, with opposite shape preferences in local and carried multirange
models. Robustness across poses/geometry is weak, and some failures survive
pruning to corridors. The two fixed mechanism contexts provide real but limited
evidence of harmful nonlocal input: zeroing it rescues128 short paired conditions
per Branch target,64 unique inputs, without rescuing prefix4+ or attachment4.
The local exception's successful pairs need its pulse and endpoint-aware halt;
the68-gene draw instead needs static flags. The shared ancestor also remains a
useful control, with more normal paired successes here than its descendants.
No consistent evolution/capacity advantage or net multirange benefit follows.

The next useful evolutionary question is:

> Can selection for consistent paired behaviour across matched shapes and poses
> retain these useful mechanisms and spread them to new branching contexts?

Prefer a single **robustness-pressure** intervention before adding capacity or
changing the runtime. Keep genome capacity, mutation policy, curriculum input
budget, number of organisms, stopping runtime and local/multirange arms fixed.
Compare the current pair-count policy with a prespecified context-first policy
that rewards complete goal pairs across poses before additional isolated wins.
Both arms should see exactly the same input batches and receive the same number
of candidate calls. Include unchanged checkpoint controls and random resampling.
Keep fresh random-origin founders in parallel with carried states; never insert
a known solver into any lineage. Preserve whole populations and RNG states.

Before registering that evolution study:

1. Freeze a J-informed, performance-independent control panel drawn from these
   retained identities; preserve distinct origins and parent/ancestor controls.
   Any J-driven choice must be labelled J-informed, never independent evidence.
2. On NEW software fixtures, calibrate a balanced generator with both straight
   and turning goal arms, near/far sources, bent prefixes, matched pruned corridors,
   extra junctions and genuine alternative-route cycles. Require known-control
   correctness and usable random-origin near-skill coverage; if it fails, record
   the failed fixtures and revise before registration. Exclude every generated
   fixture thereafter. This fixes the weak near-success anchor that limited J.
3. Include normal versus nonlocal-zero configurations as fixed diagnostic
   controls on an independent fresh comparison. Keep read charges, avoid selecting
   their best cases, and measure both rescue and lost long-prefix capabilities.
   External zeroing is not itself an evolved regulatory mechanism. Require rescue
   across several independent contexts, both poses/shapes, and explicit retention
   before interpreting it as a promising range-regulation target.
4. Define the context-first rank and corridor/diversity protection before search;
   keep the common evaluation corpus and total calls identical. Do not introduce
   both a new heritable range gate and a new rank in the same causal comparison.
5. Register a12-minute matched pilot and a numerical fresh-validation gate. Only
   a reproducible improvement across at least two independent replicates, with
   corridor/previous-skill retention, should justify60–90minutes. Save failed
   cohorts and useful diversity; do not discard the fresh-random track because
   a pilot is negative. Profile each model class and weight timings by planned
   calls, correcting J's pooled-calibration underprediction.

If the nonlocal rescue replicates but robustness pressure does not improve
retention/generalization, a separate later experiment can compare a bounded
heritable range gate against fixed exposure, with mutation/capacity costs made
explicit. This is a concrete regulation hypothesis justified by an intervention,
not evidence that extra regulation or compute will necessarily work.

Forced shortest frames in the fresh local model remain explanatory observations.
Do not retune its stopping rule using J traces or count them as normal outputs.
Any stopping intervention needs its own frozen protocol and fresh cases. No
further candidate inference is authorized by this proposal document itself.
