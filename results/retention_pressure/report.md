# M10 rotating retention-exposure comparison: audited results

The fixed primary comparison yields **0/6 integrated nominees passing full integration**, with rotating-exposure support **False** under the registered three-pair rule. These counts concern six nominee roles, not independent origins. Search completed 128 matched rounds and stopped for `round_ceiling`.

## Interpretation and next recommendation

M10 gives **no support for the registered rotating-exposure advantage**. All three
primary paired comparisons are 0–0, all 18 nomination roles fail full integration,
and none passes the separate deeper, long-ordinary or combined gate. Every nominee
solves zero of the 96 nonfork prefix2+ target pairs. No selected nominee adds a
complete pair beyond the protected union, or a new 8+edge ordinary base beyond it.
There are 79 queried ordinary bases of length16+; none of the nominees solves one.
The 19-edge M9 success was on a different panel: these raw totals are not a loss
or gain comparison across phases.

The six integrated nominees all descend from the older **shared_child** reference,
not the two gated clone founders. Their 128/400 pairs, 200/200 corridors, 7/256
ordinary bases, 168/168 correct disconnections and zero false no-path exactly match
that reference's successful-case sets on the SAME fresh panel. They retain all
96 fixed quota-parent pairs and all 32 specialist-only pairs, but only **4/22** of
the fixed parent's ordinary successes: 18 losses and 3 additions, seven total.
This is inherited specialized competence, not six new integrated solvers. Five
nominees have one M10 mutation birth and the sixth four. Five also match the
shared reference's terminal mask/status/steps on all 1,680 inputs; the remaining
control1 nominee matches 1,294, with no gain in successful cases. Distinct genomes
and nonzero ancestry depth do not establish functional progress. These saved-output
comparisons are in interpretation_evidence.json, without new inference.

The common nomination panel had just **one** fixed-parent-successful ordinary base
among its 24 ordinary bases. All primary nominees retained it. Fresh confirmation
contained 22 such parental successes and exposed the loss of 18. The failure is
therefore more informative than a flat primary score: the selection/nomination
measurement can accept a severe ordinary-skill tradeoff. We deliberately kept
that panel fixed to isolate training exposure, and this result exposes that
limitation. It does not prove that the panel alone caused the evolutionary result,
or that a larger panel would produce a general routing mechanism.

Both curricula delivered substantial external retention examples: 350 parent-
solvable ordinary bases in the control and 342 under rotation, out of 1,536 bases
per arm. Rotation changed the distribution rather than increasing the number of
successful retention examples. Its 342 comprise 122 P11 short, 121 P15 short,
46 R9 short, 41 R15 short, 3 P11 long, 8 P15 medium and 1 R9 medium. Its R15 long
stratum supplied no parental successes. Control supplied 244 P11 short, 99 R9
short and 7 P11 long. Per-round retention is transient; it is not a permanent
constraint on later descendants or nominees. Training rotation therefore did not
repair the weak persistent retention test. The three populations in each arm
share these cases; do not count them as three independent exposure samples.

The gated lineages were not simply starved. Their combined mutation births were
2,265/1,200 (control/rotating pair0), 1,646/2,813 (pair1), and 959/1,475 (pair2),
from 5,760 mutation births per population. Shared-child descendants received
813/618, 747/802 and 663/734 respectively. Broad mutation histories existed, while
selected primary descendants were shallow and inherited the older repertoire.
There were 1,120 genotypic no-op births in total, charged normally. Saved diagnostics
retain per-root depth histograms and live/archive persistence; none is evidence
that depth alone translates into solving.

One distinct secondary genotype is worth preserving, with its costs explicit:
control1 g128/b35 and rotating2 g32/b7 independently sampled the same single
C6-node duplication from the exact gated-specialist founder. The resulting hash
is `103134af609b10c444fb`; these are two aliases of one genotype and related origins,
not two independent algorithms. It solves **one new random9 maze of four edges**
in both poses beyond all twelve protected references. Relative to its own root,
it gains three ordinary bases and loses six, retains 48/128 pairs and 44/166
corridors, and preserves all 168 correct disconnections with zero false no-path.
Its 15 ordinary successes are therefore a useful narrow change with large costs,
not retained acquisition or an arm advantage. It has no new long ordinary skill.
Other ordinary-role nominees trade away still more routing competence. One loses
all 168 correct no-path answers without making false reachable no-path claims;
absence of false claims alone is not competent disconnection handling. Some
single-input successes beyond the union do not survive the paired/both-pose
criteria; those remain visible in saved_diagnostics.json, not promoted as skills.

**Recommendation: first repair and validate the selection measurement, then decide
on further evolution.** A separately registered, bounded calibration should build
a fresh, diverse retention bank with explicit nonempty successful-case counts for
the preserved references, spanning their demonstrated size/family/length skills.
Compare the current ranking with a bank-based retention test on a frozen diagnostic
pool, including these known tradeoff lineages. It must actually reject large
ordinary-skill losses before serving as the gate for another search. If sufficient
parent-solvable examples cannot be obtained within declared draw bounds, report
that rather than weakening denominators. Bank construction informed by reference
performance would be explicit external fitness design, not an unbiased estimate
of general maze performance; a separate untouched test distribution remains essential.

This proposal does not reuse inspected confirmation layouts, reselect M10 nominees,
or inject a known solver. It tests whether our measurements can distinguish the
integration we want before adding another exposure or mutation change. General
solving still needs a deeper route-selection mechanism: retaining the existing
specialists would be useful but is not that mechanism. The present C6-only operator
is a local search around an inherited computation; this experiment cannot rule out
broader representational change, other mutation destinations, longer well-measured
search or random-origin evolution in general. Scaling this exact setup now is
not justified by its observed results. No new experiment has been launched.
