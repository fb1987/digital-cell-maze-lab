# M28 integration-accessibility assay

All three arms failed the registered integration test. No proposal solved both poses of any of the four reused complementary bases, even before retention gates. See the fixed fresh nominees below for independent transfer results.

This is a bounded sexual-variation neighborhood assay around preserved random-origin lineages, with no evolutionary generations or selection-driven reproduction.

## Frozen design

Registration `f6e511e` (386 hashes), all parent material, operators and the full pool were pushed before development inference. Choice freeze `e15f4bf` was pushed before fresh case generation; all nomination decisions replayed exactly.

All 38 distinct complementary genotype pairs from M27, 15 parental genotypes, both recipient directions and eight draws per direction: 608 proposals per arm, 1,824 total. All 208 M27 aliases include 205 noncontrol historical comparison aliases and three intact controls. Parent aliases are selected by a fixed lexicographic rule among identical genotypes; all aliases remain recorded. The M27 source ancestry closure and original histories are preserved. These related forks do not constitute independent origins.

MUTATION uses one existing ordinary edit. SPLICE exchanges one contiguous homologous writer segment with its ports/initialization. REGULATED imports a homologous private expression DAG into one recipient writer, alpha-renames registers, rebinds physical ports, and appends an ordinary random local expression plus SELECT. Every operator has one opportunity; edit size and material differ. No fitness/oracle/case ID enters inference or variation.

The existing ten-channel M18 VM and limits are unchanged: 256 instructions, 31 nodes per writer, 256 ticks, 4,096 reads per site and 32,768 dispatches per site. Both branches of fused expressions consume real dispatch/read work. A fusion may be rejected rather than retried.

## A narrow secondary change, with an important limit

The regulated nominee added **12 correct individual inputs** beyond the entire historical comparison portfolio on the fresh panel. These are structured **fork** cases with prefixes 4 or 8 and route lengths 6, 10 or 11 edges, in poses 2 and 4. Every corresponding alternative-side input still fails with an emitted disconnected output. Consequently there are **zero new complete paired discriminations**, zero new corridor responses and zero new complete ordinary 8+ bases. This is an exploratory change in one-sided behavior, not a successful general routing mechanism. The other two nominees add no individual input beyond the portfolio.

The regulated nominee solves 20/480 ordinary bases in both poses, including three 8+ bases; its longest correct ordinary route is 12 edges. Mutation solves 27/480, including six 8+ bases, also at most 12 edges. The splice no-op solves 13/480, none 8+. All three are zero on the 182 ordinary 16+ bases. The fresh panel has 421 ordinary 8+ bases beyond existing portfolio coverage, so the new-skill endpoint is nonvacuous.

The regulated child retains all ordinary-base and complete-pair successes of **both actual parents** on this panel, but retains only 10/38 of the donor's correct corridor inputs. Against its older quota-parent reference it retains 48/96 pairs, 122/200 corridors and 20/27 ordinary bases. Correct disconnection remains 168/168, with zero false no-path. This distinguishes partial combination and historical recovery from complete retention. The mutation nominee retains both actual matched sources' successes, but still loses older quota-child skills; its strict root/entry gate also fails. Full gains/losses are in summary.json.

A static reading of the regulated genome shows what was composed: writer C6 contains 15 recipient nodes, 14 imported donor nodes, one condition and one SELECT, exactly 31 nodes. The condition is saturating subtraction of the first imported donor node's value minus the local goal flag. A positive result selects the donor expression's final value; otherwise it selects the recipient's. The other writers and shared persistent state follow the recipient. This describes the executable genotype; terminal outputs alone do not establish why a particular maze succeeds.

## Interpretation and next recommendation

The result weakens the specific claim that a single generic writer splice or local regulated fusion readily combines these preserved specialists. All 38 complementary pairs were included, most fusions were admitted, and even the four already-used targets remained unsolved as complete two-pose tasks. This is more informative than a result erased only by conservative retention gates. It supplies no reason yet to prefer these sexual-variation operators over ordinary mutation for ordinary maze solving.

It does **not** establish that recombination, regulation or random-origin evolution cannot work. We tested eight one-opportunity draws per parent direction, one donor segment or one fused writer at a time, without subsequent selection, tuning or multigeneration search. Crossover's 608 proposals collapsed to only 156 distinct genotypes; most parental material is shared. All target pairs were selected from related lineages and four perfect-maze examples. These limits matter, especially for cyclic mazes and for evolution through initially unhelpful intermediates.

The architectural question is now sharper. Importing two expressions does not preserve two parental reaction processes: their computations read a **single shared, evolving channel state**. Their useful behaviors may depend on different earlier state trajectories, initialization, stopping behavior or other writers. A local SELECT cannot automatically reconcile those dependencies. Shared-state interference is a plausible explanation, not a causal finding from this assay. The 12 new fork responses show that the new gate can alter behavior, but not that it has constructed the missing solver mechanic.

I recommend a **small, separately registered mechanism-and-modularity study before another long search**. Start with static dependency cones and passive traces on these now-used development cases to locate the first conflicting channel or transition. Then test whether a generic module embedding can preserve each parent's useful process and expose an affordable local coupling point. Measure required state width and work rather than assume extra channels or insulation are free. Preserve exact random-origin ancestry and a matched unchanged-runtime control; keep any future mechanism choices separate from a later fresh holdout. No oracle-controlled switching or hand-written solver should enter breeding.

A longer sexual evolution run could still be worthwhile once that substrate is shown to make at least a small combination accessible. M28 alone does not justify closing random-origin discovery or claiming that more compute would solve the architectural problem. No further experiment, expanded VM, cloud run or M29 has been launched.
