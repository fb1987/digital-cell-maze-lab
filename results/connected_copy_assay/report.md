# M18: initialized connected-copy assay

This is a fixed mechanical experiment on two preserved random-origin genomes. It tests whether generic duplication can preserve an existing computation while connecting its copy to an existing reader. It is not an evolutionary search, and designed interventions are not acquired skills.

Registration `66901ab` was pushed before generation. The entire 576-alias proposal panel and all reference choices were pushed together at `1393e36` before any fresh scientific cases. There was no nomination, adaptive selection or reselection. All 309 distinct models received 712 development and 1,680 fresh final inputs.

## Interpretation

M18 validates a useful form of growth: a generic copy can inherit its donor's initialized process, take over an existing input, and preserve the old computation while remaining capable of influencing it. Twenty-four connected-copy variants preserve mask, status and completed steps on every final input and show an original-channel change when their copied writer is zeroed. All 72 initialized connected variants preserve every final raster. This resolves the mechanical concern raised by M17, where most copies had no potential route to the output. It establishes an available mechanism; whether evolution can exploit it remains open.

Connection itself adds no measured behavioral penalty here: every initialized connected copy has exactly the same recorded reaction fields, including work, as its matched initialized disconnected copy on all 1,680 final inputs. Their zero-writer interventions differ sharply: 72/72 connected variants affect original-channel state on a sampled shared tick, versus 0/72 disconnected variants. This is evidence for actual attachment, rather than merely counting extra channel names in a genome. Sixty-eight connected zero-writer interventions also change a final mask on at least one final input.

The cost matters. The twelve initialized connected variants with lost correct responses all lose correct disconnection decisions: the anchor's `no_path` becomes `read_budget`, with four or twenty losses per affected variant. The same losses occur in their disconnected counterparts. The child copies retain all correct responses but finish fewer ticks on 128 inputs already limited by reads. These results do not justify treating equal rasters as equal completed answers, or relaxing the registered budget. They show why growth must coexist with instruction pruning, regulation and retention pressure.

Inheritance also matters in a finite subset of connections. Of 56 seed/zero contrasts with initially nonzero donors (28 connected, 28 disconnected), twelve change at least one final mask/status/step field. Initially zero donors provide no seed contrast. The two unseeded connected variants with correct-response gains are an explicit failure mode: each gains 168 correct disconnection labels while losing 594 correct reachable responses and making 1,512 false no-path claims. Neither passes the combined gate. These are not promising solver discoveries.

## Next recommendation

Proceed next to a separately registered 60–90 minute matched evolutionary comparison using this validated generic operator. Use the common M18 representation in all arms, matched preserved random-origin material and future RNG histories, equal evaluation allocations, and explicit physical work accounting. Compare ordinary generic variation with initialized connected duplication; a disconnected initialized-copy control can test the value of attachment. Sample eligible edges prospectively rather than selecting only the 24 fully neutral edges after inspecting this final panel.

Preserve complementary specialists, checkpoints and fresh random immigrants. Reward new upstream or longer ordinary routing only alongside declared retention of existing paths and correct disconnection, and retain the same hard execution caps. The goal is to see whether duplicated, attached state can diverge into a useful new process without erasing the old repertoire. M18 supplies a workable substrate and a measured cost; it supplies no guarantee of a general solver. No subsequent evolution has been launched.
