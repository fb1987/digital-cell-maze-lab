# M9 conditional-regulation evolution: audited results

The fixed primary comparison yields **0/6 integrated nominees passing full integration**, with regulation support **False** under the registered three-pair rule. These counts concern six nominee roles, not independent origins. Search completed 128 matched rounds and stopped for `round_ceiling`.

## What was tested

Three paired populations per arm, six populations of 96. Each pair began with identical ordered genomes and RNG:43 clones each of exact M7 quota0 child and its immediate parent, plus ten protected references including the shared0 repair. M9 inherited these random-origin lineages explicitly; it did not insert a known solver or promote an M8 intervention. Histories share origins.

The regulation arm used a 50/50 mixture of a uniformly chosen valid gate for the last C6 writer and the unchanged generic mutation operator forced to destination block 6. Global used the unchanged M1 whole-genome operator. Both produced 45 mutation births and 7 random immigrants per round, with 44 survivors and 16 archive slots. The M1 runtime remained unchanged. The combined operator changes both locality and gate frequency; this is not a gate-only causal test.

Both arms used a new common integration-oriented ranking, finite-batch neutral reserve, coverage preservation and lexicase. Ranking favored no false disconnection, minimum nonempty fixed-parent skill retention, specialist-only pair coverage, mean retention, then the existing domain-balanced score. Neutral intermediates could still survive and reproduce. Both curricula mixed short-route retention tasks with longer challenges. Thus only the operator differs between arms, while comparisons to M7 also involve common selection/curriculum changes.

Nominal allocations are identical:96×48 queries per training round and 115×392 per assessment per population, including nonselectable A0 padding. Identical genotype/query work is physically deduplicated. Equal logical budgets are not equal CPU. Fresh confirmation has 1680 inputs:400 complete pairs,200 corridors,256 ordinary bases in two poses,168 disconnected. All prior and setup/search layouts were excluded. Familiar grammars and correlated poses limit generalization claims.

Registration `7d1199a` was pushed before search. Exact choices, roots, parents, ancestry and final states were pushed as `d7e2862` BEFORE fresh confirmation generation. The fixed integrated, upstream-exploratory and ordinary roles were not changed on final data.

## Interpretation of the actual result

**The full integration test failed, but conditional regulation did evolve.**
All six primary scores are zero and the prespecified operator-support rule fails.
There is no demonstrated arm advantage. One regulation lineage nevertheless
provides a useful, freshly evaluated partial result; it must not be promoted to a
full retention success simply because its aggregate ordinary count stayed equal.

Exact `m9/modular/0/regulation/g62/b51` solves 112 pairs/200 corridors/17 ordinary bases,
with 168 correct disconnections and zero false no-path. Against the fixed parent it
retains all 96 paired skills and all 200 corridors, and adds16 of32 specialist pairs,
all prefix 0 cycle/second-junction cases across four templates. But it retains only
14 of17 parent ordinary bases: three are lost and three different ones gained.
Development had shown complete ordinary retention on only 4 parent-successful bases among 24 ordinary bases; that
claim did not transfer. Equal17/256 totals hide the failures. Its new nine-edge
perfect 11 base is absent from all ten protected references.

Against its OWN exact founder (the quota specialist, not the fixed integration
parent), this same descendant gains 46 corridors and 6 ordinary bases with no old
ordinary loss, but gains 4 fork pairs while losing16 prefix1 cycle/second-junction
pairs. Against its immediate one-edit parent it gains 44 corridors and 4 ordinary
bases, with 16 paired gains and 16 paired losses. These are different comparisons,
not interchangeable measures of progress.

Two M9 mutations are responsible for the changed source program: generation 61
changed the final C6 gate from ALWAYS to existing scratch register 18; generation 62
swapped two C6 input ports. Static reduction of the unchanged VM's instructions
gives, for a completed update at an open site:

```
C6(next) = direction-2 neighbor's C3, if C1 > 0 and C5 > 4
           0, otherwise
```

The first gate edit used `C1 > 4 and C5 > 0`; the port swap exchanged those roles.
The threshold 4 and intermediate arithmetic were already present in the inherited
block. Evolution chose an existing computation as the condition and rebound its
inputs; no human supplied that condition. These channels are internal state,
not oracle distances. This is a static output-expression derivation, not a new
trajectory experiment, universal routing proof or claim that only these nodes
consume work. Other executed nodes and gates still affect read/dispatch budgets.
The nine-edge novelty is already present in the one-edit parent on this same
confirmation panel; it is not attributed solely to the last port swap.

The gated nominee still has 106 instructions. Its conservative static output/halt
cone expands from 14 potential nodes in its exact founder to 18; this count includes
conditional alternatives that may not matter to the output predicate. This is
activation of inherited computational capacity, not growth of raw genome length.

The next descendant, the separately frozen ordinary-role nominee `g63/b27`,
changes that gate again. It retains all 32 specialist-only pairs and all 96 fixed-parent
pairs, but solves only 166/200 corridors and 14 ordinary bases. Relative to `g62/b51`,
it loses 34 corridors and 3 ordinary bases. Thus the nominated chain demonstrates a
real regulation/retention tradeoff, not monotonic accumulation of every skill.

A different regulation replicate's ordinary nominee `g127/b40` has five ordinary
successes beyond the protected union (lengths6,8,8,4,4; three perfect and two random).
It solves 15 ordinary bases but only 88 corridors, retains 10/17 fixed-parent ordinary
bases, and retains none of the32 target specialist pairs. Its first mutation changes
the C6 neighbor direction; the second gates the writer on register 8, the VM's
constant1. That second edit is functionally unconditional, so these successes are
not evidence that every promising regulation-arm genome learned conditional use.
The individual longer-route and integration gates still fail.

All 18 nominee roles solve0/96 targeted nonforkprefix2+ pairs. No nominee passes the
upstream, long-ordinary or combined gate. A19-edge perfect 11 success seen in
`g62/b51` and `g63/b27` is already solved by their exact quota-child founder on these
same fresh cases. It is inherited capability, not a newly acquired19-edge solver;
M8's different panel cannot establish when it arose. No new16+base beyond the
protected union was acquired by any nominee. The queried16+denominator is86.

**Recommended next step, proposal only:** preserve these exact gated lineages and
run a bounded comparison of broader, rotating retention pressure before a long
campaign. Keep the existing runtime and a matched unchanged-pressure control,
use new excluded-layout-safe cases, and retain the separate deeper-prefix endpoint.
The specific hypothesis is that a broader retention sample can distinguish useful
context-sensitive integration from the ordinary-skill substitutions missed by the
small development panel. Fresh confirmation must remain untouched until choices
are frozen. This result supports continued random-origin regulation research;
it does not justify declaring the retention bottleneck solved or simply extending
M9 unchanged. No new phase is launched.
