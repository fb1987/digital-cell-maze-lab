## Reviewed interpretation

**Safe growth was genuinely exercised.** The disabled arm admitted8,081extensions and the enabled arm8,051. Their current descendants persisted across selection and reproduction, not just single unselected edits. Across all arms,98,415mutants and15,309random immigrants were produced. The longest final live/archive lineages reached26new mutation births in disabled populations,27in enabled populations and12in ordinary populations. Up to seven admitted extensions occurred on a surviving disabled chain; final genomes included up to six recognizable wrappers. These are M23 study depths from preserved entries, not all-time generations from the first random genome.

**There was opportunity to edit the new material.** Disabled populations generated1,534activation-expression edits and1,525payload-expression edits; enabled populations generated1,828and1,883. Among children with a subsequent training exposure,648/1,523disabled activation edits and743/1,817enabled activation edits survived that next selection. Extension births survived1,871/8,058disabled and1,938/8,023enabled. At the end,133disabled-arm and167enabled-arm live/archive individuals had extension ancestry. This establishes persistence and opportunities for adaptation. It does not establish that a selector ever enabled useful payload on relevant cases, or that added branches caused a gain. Related, diverged histories and different selected parents also prevent reading these survival percentages as a clean causal policy effect.

**The conservative insertion limit was not repeatedly blocking most attempted insertions in this run.** Posthoc event-schema counts show66/8,147disabled append attempts and136/8,187enabled attempts were bounded no-ops. That observation applies to the parents actually presented here; it does not establish that the128-instruction admission limit would be sufficient for a much deeper campaign. The common VM still permits256instructions through ordinary edits, and one final population contained a138-instruction individual. More heritable capacity remains an experimental question, not a conclusion from this run.

**The negative primary result is not just the90%retention threshold.** All27frozen nominee roles have zero raw new target pairs beyond the declared archive/root/entry union, before applying their retention gates. All six distinct nominees solved0/79ordinary bases with paths of at least16edges in both registered poses. Lowering a retention threshold would not create missing target novelty. This result is about this bounded search and its tested domain; it does not prove that random-origin solver evolution is impossible.

### What changed in the three evolved nominees

The disabled primary, `m23/connected/0/disabled/g80/b40`, has four M23 mutation births: activate a spare node, append one disabled branch, replace one output-expression node, then edit the activation expression. It retains every correct response of its exact root on fresh confirmation, including168correct no-path answers, but acquires none. Its mask/status/tick profile differs from archive profiles while its set of correct inputs matches the old A0/quota-parent set. Changed code and changed rasters are therefore not a solving advance.

The enabled exploratory nominee, `m23/connected/0/enabled/g126/b15`, adds40complete pairs relative to its exact root but loses20, adds one ordinary base and loses six. It preserves168correct no-path answers, yet retains only82.76%of root pair skills and14.29%of root ordinary skills. None of its successful individual inputs is beyond the historical comparison union. These are genuine within-lineage skill changes on the same fresh cases, not a new portfolio capability or integrated solver.

The enabled primary, `m23/connected/0/enabled/g190/b50`, is more interesting mechanistically: its16-birth chain contains two admitted extensions, three activation-expression edits, five payload-expression edits, three port swaps, two operand edits and one node duplication. Its113-instruction final genome retains two recognizable wrappers. Relative to its exact `gated_partial` root, it gains six complete pairs and four ordinary bases, while losing14pairs and one ordinary base. Pair retention is87.5%, ordinary retention95.45%; all168correct no-path answers are retained with zero false claims. These gains are mostly skills already held by other archive members; the only new individual success beyond that archive is the case below. A long chain with wrapper edits is evidence that heritable material was modified and retained, not evidence that those wrappers caused its correct routing.

### Controls, scope and the general-solver hypothesis

Frozen A0/quota-parent references on the SAME confirmation cases achieved96complete pairs,200corridor answers,25/256ordinary bases in both poses,168/168correct no-path answers and zero false no-path. The preserved M15 specialist achieved184pairs,200corridors,11ordinary bases and0correct no-path. Those are inherited reference abilities, not M23 acquisitions. Intact controls and conventional BFS/A*/bitwave checks were all correct. No candidate speed advantage was established.

The comparison archive is the prospectively declared103aliases/73genotypes, plus protected references and exact root/entry comparisons. It is not every mutant ever generated, and it does not include all of M22's own later candidate pool. Claims of novelty are limited to that declared set. Related sources and familiar maze-generator families also limit generalization. Fresh random immigration here is not a separate, independently randomized from-scratch population arm.

My assessment is that we have now validated an important engineering prerequisite—safe heritable extension with subsequent edits under selection—but have not shown the central scientific step: accumulating complementary rules into a more general shortest-path algorithm. This is a stronger negative than an unselected short splice assay, because substantial reproduction and modification actually occurred. It is still only243population rounds and a maximum observed final lineage depth of27new mutation births, so it cannot settle the value of thousands of generations or a different capacity/pressure regime.

### Recommended next step — proposal only

Before another large search, run a small, separately registered causal audit of the frozen16-birth enabled lineage. Compare cost-matched branch bypasses, its earlier ancestors and the final output-writer port swap on already-used cases, explicitly as development/mechanism data. This asks whether evolved regulation contributes to the new six-edge response or whether generic port changes account for it while wrappers persist as passengers. It should also check retained correct-disconnection behavior and losses, rather than judging only the single success.

Preserve the exact ending populations, archives and RNG for any later sustained continuation. If the branches make a useful causal contribution, that supplies a better basis for a longer continued search. If they do not, first improve how generic variation exposes cooperating computations; simply requesting10,000generations of this same configuration would not yet address the observed missing integration. This recommendation keeps random-origin discovery open and does not authorize or launch M24, additional evolution, recombination, solver injection or cloud compute.
