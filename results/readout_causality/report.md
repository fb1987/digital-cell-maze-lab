# M21: the short-maze gain comes from changing the readout

**The copied process is not required for this gain when its reader is reconnected to the original donor.** Changing the original parent’s C6 terminal alone reproduces the gained four-edge maze in both poses. Restoring the old terminal removes that success in all four saved specialists. The terminal-only model loses 252 original correct responses while gaining 34; only two gained inputs are beyond all thirteen protected references.

A historical check also identifies the minimal appended-terminal genome as an EXACT match, after standard empty-spare embedding, to two saved M10 nominees. M20 rediscovered this mechanism in new variants and on new cases. M21 provides causal attribution; it does not establish a new evolved solver mechanism or new confirmation evidence.

For every specialist: saved versus restored original terminal crossed with intact copy, reader bypass with copy retained, copy removal without bypass, and bypass plus removal. Bypass swaps the reader’s live copy port with the dormant original donor port. Removal clears the copied writer and its seed. Terminal restoration adapts only the destination scratch address. Full original-reader restoration and terminal-only append/replace controls distinguish expression, port and instruction-count effects. All thirteen protected references, exact anchors/copy bases and intact flat/M1/M18 controls were evaluated on identical cases.

No generations, nominations, fresh cases, solver injection, mating or runtime extensions were used. The bounded M18 VM is unchanged.

## What the terminal is doing

The original parent already computed the subtraction internally. The shared duplicate-node event appended that existing private expression as the C6 writer’s final value, replacing which expression becomes visible to other channels. It did not invent a new arithmetic primitive. For the minimal original-parent variant, the expression simplifies to:

```text
C6[t+1,p] = (C1[t,p] > 0) × max(0, C5[t,p] − 4)
```

Three saved specialists use C1 directly for that condition; generic d3 uses its copied C1 signal in C8. The thresholded magnitude comes from C5. The output raster is still C2, which responds through the inherited recurrent network; C6 is an intermediate signal, not the final path mask. The four-edge route length was not encoded in a genome or supplied to inference. The constant four was already present in the ancestral expression. Success on this particular maze does not turn that threshold into a general shortest-path procedure.

This posthoc recurrence matches ALL 110,480 open-site, completed-tick transitions across the four specialists and eight preregistered trace cases. All four bypass-plus-removal comparisons preserve the original eight channel states at every shared completed tick on all eight cases: 32/32 comparisons. For all specialists, restoring the old terminal changes those states on six cases and preserves them on the two selected disconnected cases. The full histories, including their different lifetimes, are retained.

On the gained maze, the output is empty through tick6, begins with one lit site at tick7, has two at tick8 and emits the exact five-site/four-edge path at tick9. This observation explains the actual bounded reaction, not a general algorithm or behaviour after forced continuation. No incomplete interrupted tick is counted as a history state.

## Historical identity and research implication

The terminal-only appended genome is exactly the standard M18 embedding of M10 control1 g128/b35 and rotating2 g32/b7. historical_matches.json records both complete genome comparisons and verifies the old choices file against its original seal. No old study was rerun. M10 already reported the same C6 duplication, a short ordinary gain and losses of paired/corridor skills. The M20 cases were fresh at M20 evaluation, so their measured capability remains valid; algorithmic novelty does not follow from succeeding on another fresh case. Repeated discovery across these related lineages is not evidence of cumulative construction.

This result shifts the immediate emphasis from adding whole copied processes to preserving and composing alternative readouts. The arithmetic needed for the short specialist was already in the scaffold; evolution exposed it by changing the writer’s terminal expression, at a large retention cost. It remains possible that copies help other mutations or longer searches; M21 addresses these four backgrounds and this mechanism only. Random-origin solver discovery remains open.

My next recommendation is a separately registered, bounded accessibility test of a **generic guarded-expression extension**: retain the old expression as an addressable branch while adding a mutable alternative and mutable local selector, compared with the present replacement grammar. Existing M9 gate-only work changed the activation of one readout; this would test retention of both expression alternatives. The selector must use only ordinary internal/local state, never case IDs, oracle routes or a rule choosing whichever completed mask grades better. Require retained skills and fresh gains, then decide whether sustained evolution is justified. Do not count recovered protected skills or another four-edge rediscovery as a general-solver milestone. This is a proposal only; no next phase or long run is running.

The VM already contains SELECT and can express these alternatives. The proposed change would concern how generic heritable extensions are generated, not adding a shortest-path primitive or proving the present representation incapable. Any favorable neutral initialization must be declared and compared fairly; it must not install a hand-selected maze-specific switching rule.
