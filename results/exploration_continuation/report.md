M15 confirmed a new, limited routing capability in one random-origin descendant. It did not produce a solver that combines that capability with reliable disconnection handling and ordinary-maze performance. The registered primary comparison remains zero against zero in all three pairs. The specialist gain is scientifically useful even though every strict acquisition gate fails.

## What sustained exploration taught us

All six primary nominees are inherited M14 genomes with zero M15 mutations. Their terminal masks, statuses and steps match the fixed parent on all 1,680 confirmation inputs. Their 13/256 ordinary successes therefore establish no new M15 acquisition. Raw totals from the earlier M14 confirmation used different cases and are not a gain/loss comparison.

The exploration mechanism did operate: 15,649 explorer presentations represented 7,686 identities, and 15,660 offspring used reserved explorer breeding slots. Of presentations with a next training round, 7,988/15,613 explorers survived; 3,369/15,624 reserved offspring survived. These repeated training observations show preservation and reproduction of alternatives. They do not establish that the alternatives were useful algorithmic stepping stones.

The confirmed specialist arose through the shared-lexicase breeding path, directly from a protected reference, with one port swap in block 0. It did not descend through a long chain of the reserved explorer offspring. Its 106 instructions contain 15 potentially relevant primitive nodes under static dependency analysis; that analysis is not a temporal or causal trace. Although final live/archive lineages reach cumulative M14+M15 mutation depths of 10–24, the new confirmed capability is a one-edit discovery. Longer population history supplied more opportunities to discover it; this run does not demonstrate progressively accumulated algorithmic complexity or an exploration-policy advantage. The three already-diverged paired histories and the failed fixed primary endpoint further limit causal conclusions.

## Research recommendation

Keep this specialist and all population histories. The next useful question is whether its upstream routing and the lost skills can coexist in the current runtime. I recommend a separately registered, bounded mechanism and restoration study around the exact specialist and genetic parent, followed by a small matched continuation only if the evidence warrants it. Diagnose the changed channel dependencies and disconnected failures; compare generic variants under explicit routing-plus-retention objectives with frozen references and an unchanged-search control. Freeze all choices before new confirmation, and require preservation of the newly acquired upstream pairs together with restored disconnection and ordinary skills. Include longer prefixes and ordinary length strata so a narrow restoration cannot masquerade as generalization.

This is a proposal, not a launched M16. New traces, perturbations or cases require the next protocol. Avoid hand-written route cleanup or a known solver inside the lineage. Recombination remains a later option if component compatibility is demonstrated. If restoration repeatedly removes the routing gain, that would motivate a precise test of regulatory interference or evolvability; the present evidence alone does not prove an architectural impossibility. Random-origin discovery remains viable as a research hypothesis, with positive evidence for narrow acquisitions and unresolved evidence for accumulating them into a general solver.
