## Reviewed interpretation

The primary result is stronger than a failure to meet the 90% retention cutoff:
**all 18 frozen roles have zero raw novel ordinary 8+ bases before gates**, zero
new target upstream pairs, and zero new individual successes beyond the declared
100-model archive plus protected references. Six role aliases pass the retention,
disconnection and phase-attribution gate, so eligible candidates existed. Removing
the retention gate would not create missing novelty. The 18 roles are only five
distinct nominees, including three M25 descendants; these are correlated results.

All five nominees solve zero of the 86 ordinary bases with shortest paths of at
least 16 edges in both poses. Their longest correctly completed two-pose ordinary
route is eight edges, already present among the references. This does not establish
a scalable maze-solving procedure or comparative speed. It does identify a clear
remaining gap between success on familiar routing motifs and a general solver.

### Selection, heritable growth and an observed capacity constraint

The policy change was exercised. Every population had explorer presentations in
all 319 training rounds and produced 3,828 explorer-bred mutants. Each INTEGRATE
population additionally produced exactly 3,828 balanced-cycle mutants and 6,699
shared-lexicase mutants; CURRENT produced 10,527 shared-lexicase mutants. Each had
2,233 random immigrants. Both imperfect intermediates and the new allocation were
therefore available. This does not isolate which policy component affected any
particular lineage.

Across six populations, evolution produced 86,130 mutant births and 13,398 random
immigrants. Final live/archive maximum M25 depths range from 26 to 43 new mutation
births, including any no-ops, with up to five admitted extensions on a surviving
chain. The three evolved nominees have 145, 129 and 116 instructions and three,
four and one recognizable wrappers respectively. These measurements demonstrate
continued inheritance and modification; wrapper syntax alone does not prove useful
regulation or cumulative construction of a solving algorithm.
These are phase/source-study ancestry counts anchored at preserved references,
not the genomes' total evolutionary ages from their first random origins.

still produced no confirmed portfolio novelty. It is not established as the cause
of the negative result.

### Recommended next step — proposal only

Keep the exact populations, archives and RNG so the random-origin track can continue.
M25 supplies no descriptive support for this integration-policy package, and simply
scaling this configuration would leave the observed absence of new longer routes
unexplained. It does not prove that more evolutionary time, capacity or regulation
cannot help; 319 new population rounds and at most 43 surviving phase mutation
births are far from a universal test of those hypotheses.

My next recommendation is a saved-record evolvability audit focused on ordinary
routes of eight or more edges. Measure first-training-exposure gains, their next
selection survival and subsequent reproduction, alongside actual append headroom.
Use the already recorded rows and reference presentations, explicitly as posthoc
development evidence. This separates the questions “did useful longer-route
variants appear?” and “were they retained and bred?” and makes the capacity
observation testable without first spending another large search allocation.
Training novelty would be relative only to references evaluated on those same
training cases, not the 100-model final archive, and could not replace confirmation.

If promising longer-route variants repeatedly arise and disappear, a targeted
pressure/curriculum or retention comparison is justified. If they rarely arise,
test variation access and safe heritable capacity in the preserved backgrounds,
including neutral behavior and measured cost under dispatch limits. That decision
should precede the next sustained campaign. No saved-record audit, new evolution,
capacity change, recombination or next phase has been launched by this recommendation.
